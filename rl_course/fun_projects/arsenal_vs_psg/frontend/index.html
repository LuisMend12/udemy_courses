One caveat the numbers hide. Both tables chase a moving target, so self-play is non-stationary and different training seeds settle on different equilibria. Across five independent runs of the Python trainer, Arsenal's win rate came out 42%, 48%, 27%, 44% and 66% — a 39-point spread. This page plays one of those runs, so read it as a single opinion, not the answer.

How it works

An MDP small enough to solve exactly

The state

Zone, whether the team is under pressure, how tired it is, and whether it leads. 4 × 2 × 3 × 3 = 72 states, five actions — small enough to solve exactly, big enough for two styles to emerge.

A held-open update

After a team acts, the opponent plays before it sees another state. So each Q-update stays open until that team is next on the ball, and everything in between — conceding above all — is banked into the pending reward.

The reward is just the goal

+1 scoring, -1 conceding. Costs for giving it away or stalling are near-zero on purpose: price a turnover anywhere near a goal and both agents learn cowardice, recycling in midfield all night.

The agents kept cheating

They shot from midfield 60% of the time because a block might win a corner; they used hopeless 40-yarders as a cheap way to concede possession. Every exploit was correct play against a model that was wrong about football.

Ratings and rosters are eyeballed for flavour, not scouted. This is a toy.
